3. Inpatient Hospitalisation Conditions
Clause 3.1: Definition of Qualifying Inpatient Admission

The Daily Hospital Income Benefit is payable only where the Life Assured is admitted as a registered bed-patient in an approved restructured or private hospital upon the professional recommendation of a certified medical practitioner, and remains continuously warded for a minimum qualifying period of twenty-four (24) consecutive hours.

Clause 3.2: Kidney Stones & Acute Renal Admissions (Explicit Coverage Terms)

Does the policy cover kidney stones?
YES, FOR INPATIENT ADMISSIONS: If the Life Assured ({{insured}}) is admitted as an inpatient for the medical or surgical management of renal calculi (kidney stones), acute renal colic, ureteric calculi, or associated obstructive nephropathy and is continuously warded for at least 24 hours, the Daily Hospital Cash Benefit of SGD 250.00 per day will be paid for each full 24-hour period of hospitalisation.

IMPORTANT EXCLUSION — OUTPATIENT ESWL:
Under Section 4.3 of this Policy, day-surgery procedures, outpatient extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL), and day-clinic endoscopic stone laser procedures that do not involve an overnight hospital ward admission of 24 consecutive hours or more are strictly EXCLUDED from cash benefit payouts.

Clause 3.3: Pre-Existing Chronic Illnesses (Hypertension & Diabetes Terms)

For pre-existing hypertension and Type 2 diabetes declared at policy inception (2023-01-15), hospitalisation resulting directly from acute hypertensive crises or diabetic ketoacidosis is covered after the completion of the initial twenty-four (24) month moratorium period.

4. General Exclusions

NO DAILY HOSPITAL INCOME BENEFIT SHALL BE PAYABLE UNDER THIS POLICY IN RESPECT OF HOSPITAL CONFINEMENT DIRECTLY OR INDIRECTLY ARISING FROM:

  1. Hospitalisation shorter than twenty-four (24) consecutive hours.
  2. Outpatient medical care, emergency department treatment without admission, or outpatient day-surgeries (including outpatient ESWL kidney stone lithotripsy).
  3. Routine medical check-ups, diagnostic scans or blood investigations performed without active therapeutic medical treatment.
  4. Cosmetic surgery, weight reduction management, or aesthetic surgery.
  5. Dental procedures, dental surgery, or periodontic care.
  6. Pregnancy, childbirth, fertility treatment, miscarriage, or termination of pregnancy.
  7. Psychiatric illness, mental disorders, sleep apnoea, or stress-related conditions.
  8. Self-inflicted injury, suicide attempt, or injuries incurred while under the influence of non-prescribed narcotics or alcohol.
  9. Sexually transmitted infections, HIV, or AIDS-related illnesses.
  10. War, invasion, acts of foreign enemies, rebellion, or participation in civil commotion.
